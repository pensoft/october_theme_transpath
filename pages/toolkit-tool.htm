What does it cost you to be a steward of nature?

When answering the question, did you consider financial, social, or personal costs? Often, when we think about “cost,” we default to an economic perspective.  However, this is just one dimension.  Costs can also be social, emotional, or environmental. Moreover, the way a society defines stewardship significantly influences how these costs are calculated and prioritized. We have seen that the highest cost a society can incur arises when nature is approached solely through a conservationist lens. In many countries, conservation models have resulted in significant social, emotional, and economic burdens for millions of people. This tool aims for a stewardship approach to caring for nature. “Stewardship” is a more holistic and inclusive concept, one that acknowledges the need to support and compensate those who care for our ecosystems. Stewardship does come with financial costs, particularly in recognizing and remunerating the work of stewards.

End goal of this tool:

Prioritize stewardship over conservation by acknowledging local communities’ connection to their land and compensating ecosystem care services.

Get inspired by : Nashulai Natural Conservancy

Nashulai is a community-owned conservancy located on the edge of the Maasai Mara National Park, where financial benefits are directly allocated to local stewards. This model shows how stewardship can be both socially empowering and ecologically effective, offering a powerful alternative to traditional conservation approaches.
